What defines our treatments over others:

Dr.Wang practices Chinese Medicine and Acupuncture primarily based on the classical style which emphasizes the palpation techniques of the practitioner over what the patient may or may not tell the practitioner. This means:

  1. emphasis on pulse diagnosis and meridian diagnosis
  2. not asking too many questions

Acupuncture Therapy:

Acupuncture involves insertion of specialized fine, sterile, single-use needles into "Acupoints" around various areas of the body. It increases blood flow, reduces inflammation, relaxes tension, stimulates the body to produce natural pain killers and healing factors, calms the nerves, among other beneficial physiological effects, thereby achieving overall balance throughout the body. Infra-red light may be used in conjunction as well.

Acupuncture is near painless because the needles are so thin that at least 10 acupuncture needles can fit into 1 syringe. Many patients don't even know when the needles are inserted or withdrawn.

Acupuncture is a highly effective thousands-year-old medical modality of Oriental origin that is used today commonly for multiple types of pain conditions and various systemic imbalances, either as a stand-alone therapy or in conjunction with Chinese herbal medicine.

We use many different types of Acupuncture techniques and protocols based on the patient's condition:

  • Local Acupuncture technique
  • Trigger point Acupuncture
  • Meridian therapy Acupuncture
  • Distal needling Acupuncture
  • Scalp Acupuncture
  • 5-element balance Acupuncture
  • Tung method Acupuncture
  • Ear Acupuncture

    Cupping Therapy

    Cupping therapy involves the suctioning of broad body surface areas such as the back by creating a vacuum inside customized glass cups. The regional vacuum increases blood flow to the area and warms up the surrounding tissues.

    Cupping therapy is able to increase and improve circulation warm up affected areas stimulate healing expel stagnant blood flow promote muscular relaxation reduce pain.

    Commonly applied areas include the neck, upper and lower back, shoulder, knees and legs.
